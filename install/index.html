Ripe-O-Matic · install

Put the firmware on an ESP32 straight from this page. Nothing to install on your computer. Works in Chrome or Edge on a desktop or laptop (the browser needs USB access, which phones and Firefox/Safari don't give).

1 · Plug the ESP32 into this computer

The tested board is the ELEGOO ESP32-WROOM-32 DevKit (30-pin); any ESP32 DevKit works. Use a USB cable that carries data: many charging cables don't, and the board then never shows up. Windows plays its chime when it sees the board.

1b · Windows only: install the USB driver (30 seconds)

The ELEGOO board (and most ESP32 DevKits) talk over a CP2102 chip, and Windows usually doesn't ship its driver, so the board chimes but never appears in the port list. Mac and Linux need nothing.

  1. Download CP210x_Universal_Windows_Driver.zip (Silicon Labs, free; if that link moves, it's on this page under Downloads).
  2. Open the zip. Right-click silabser.inf (Type: Setup Information) → Install. On Windows 11, click Show more options first if you don't see Install. Say yes to the admin prompt; it ends with "The operation completed successfully".
  3. Unplug the board and plug it back in.

Already have it? Then the port list in step 2 shows a CP2102 USB to UART Bridge Controller (COMx) entry and you can skip this.

2 · Install

Click Install. A list of ports pops up: pick CP2102 USB to UART Bridge Controller (COMx) (on a Mac, cu.usbserial-…), click Connect, and wait about a minute. When it asks, type your Wi-Fi; it then links straight to your device.

Which one in the list is the board?

It's the one called CP2102 USB to UART Bridge Controller, Silicon Labs CP210x, CH340, USB Single Serial or USB-SERIAL. Ignore anything that says Bluetooth or ends in – Paired (that tag means a Bluetooth device, even if it's called "serial port"), and headsets, speakers or printers you recognise.

Board not in the list at all, even though Windows chimed when you plugged it in? Do step 1b.

Still not sure? Click Cancel, unplug the board, click Install again and look at the list: the one that disappeared is your board. Plug it back in and pick that one.

Picked the wrong one? No harm done. The installer checks it's talking to an ESP32 before it writes anything.

3 · Open it

When it asks for Wi-Fi, use your 2.4 GHz network: if your router shows two names, pick the one without "5G" in it (the ESP32 can't see 5 GHz). After the Wi-Fi step the dialog shows a Visit device button. Bookmark it, or open http://ripeomatic.local on your phone and add it to the home screen. The device page then walks you through the rest one step at a time: phone alerts, a motor direction check, the two calibrations, the first avocado.

If it doesn't go

  • No port in the list: on Windows, do step 1b. If your board isn't an ELEGOO and the chip next to its USB connector says CH340, it needs the CH340 driver instead. Otherwise try another cable or USB port.
  • "Failed to initialize" / stuck at connecting: hold the board's BOOT button, click Install, release BOOT once it starts writing.
  • Another program has the port: close the Arduino IDE serial monitor or any other terminal.
  • Skipped the Wi-Fi step, or it failed: the device starts its own network, RipeOMatic-xxxxxx. Join it from your phone and a setup page opens.
  • Something else: the Logs & console option in the dialog shows what the board prints. Paste that into an issue.
